For full-time travelers

Why nomads make South Dakota home

If you live on the road, you still need a home state for your driver's license, your vehicle and your mail. South Dakota is built for people like you.

No state income taxSouth Dakota does not tax personal income.
One night is enoughA single documented night's stay in the state, within the past year, counts toward residency for your license.
Your mail, handledA South Dakota mail-forwarding address can serve as your address, with a signed residency affidavit.

Getting a South Dakota driver's license, step by step

  1. Sign up with a South Dakota mail-forwarding service.You'll get a personal mailbox (PMB) address in the state. Keep a receipt or a piece of mail showing it, no more than a year old.
  2. Spend one night in South Dakota.A hotel, motel, campground or RV park. Keep the receipt with your name on it, dated within the past year.
  3. Go to a South Dakota driver license office.Bring your usual ID documents, the lodging receipt and the mailbox proof, and sign the state's Residency Affidavit there (the examiners can notarize it).
  4. Register your vehicles and move your life over.Title and plates, insurance, bank and voter records follow the new address. Mail-forwarding services walk you through it.
Voting has changed. A 2025 South Dakota law requires 30 consecutive days at a fixed home in the state to vote in state and local elections. Travelers who use a mailing address and have no physical home there can still receive a federal-only absentee ballot for President and Congress. Lawmakers have since moved to repeal the 30-day rule, so check the Secretary of State's page before you register.
Before you switch, check: that your health insurance works where you actually travel, what your car insurance costs with a South Dakota address, and when you'll need to be back in the state to renew your license.

Checked September 29, 2026. Laws change; confirm with the state before you rely on this. Not legal or tax advice.
