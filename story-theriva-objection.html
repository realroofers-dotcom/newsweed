Shareholder files emergency objection after Theriva got more time in under 24 hours

Theriva’s motion did not mention its written December 1 offer or the trading halt. The reverse split takes effect December 1.

Newsweed · November 25, 2026

Theriva Biologics (NYSE American: TOVX): extension requested November 23 · granted November 24, in under 24 hours · trading halted since November 23 · 1-for-30 reverse split takes effect 11:59 p.m. Eastern, December 1 · status conference set for December 7.

Mark Nejmeh, a Theriva Biologics stockholder who is suing the company and four of its directors in federal court in Nevada, filed an emergency objection on November 25 asking the District Judge to set aside an order that gave Theriva more time to answer his complaint, with no fixed end date (Nejmeh v. Theriva Biologics, Inc., No. 3:26-cv-00705, ECF No. 63).

Under 24 hours

Theriva’s request was entered the evening of November 23 and granted at midday November 24, before Mr. Nejmeh’s time to respond had run. That morning, Theriva’s lawyers had offered in writing to answer by December 1 if he gave up any further amendment; he declined. Hours later, NYSE American halted trading in TOVX for trading below ten cents. Theriva’s motion mentioned neither.

Why it is an emergency

The reverse split takes effect at 11:59 p.m. Eastern on December 1, and split-adjusted trading is to begin December 2. The court set a status conference for December 7. The objection asks that the extension be set aside and the conference be held before December 1.

The drug sold for $350,000

In October, while his request for an injunction was pending, Theriva sold SYN-004, a drug that met its Phase 2b goal and held FDA Breakthrough Therapy designation, for $350,000 upfront to Biossil, a Toronto startup the Globe and Mail described as aiming to “give failed drugs new life with AI.” SYN-004 had not failed. Mr. Nejmeh believes promising drugs are being taken cheap and relabeled as AI discoveries, delaying treatment while patients wait.

Theriva reports an accumulated deficit of approximately $365.4 million, financed “principally” by issuing stock.

“This is the most important filing, because it shows we fight and do not quit,” Mr. Nejmeh said.

What happens next

Theriva’s response to the objection is due December 9, and to his November 24 motion for reconsideration December 8. The claims are allegations; no court has ruled on them. Mr. Nejmeh’s filings and a plain-English summary are at nejmehvstheriva.com.
