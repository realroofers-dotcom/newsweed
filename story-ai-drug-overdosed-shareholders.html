The AI drug that overdosed shareholders

A Theriva Biologics stockholder says he will subpoena Wall Street funds, Michael Dell's MSD, the vote counter and the Thiel- and OpenAI-backed company that bought three Theriva drug programs for $350,000.

Newsweed · November 14, 2026

Mark Nejmeh, a Theriva Biologics (NYSE American: TOVX) stockholder who is suing the company and four of its directors in federal court in Nevada, says that once discovery opens he will seek records and testimony from the firms on the other side of Theriva's deals: Broadridge, which counted the votes; the placement agent A.G.P./Alliance Global Partners; the funds that received 16,184,560 new warrants in October 2025, including Anson, Armistice, CVI, Empery, Hudson Bay and Intracoastal; MSD, Michael Dell's investment firm; the auditor BDO USA; and Biossil, the Toronto company that bought three Theriva drug programs on October 30 for $350,000 up front.

The AI buyer

Biossil's pitch, in the words of a Globe and Mail headline, is to "give failed drugs new life with AI." The Canadian technology site BetaKit reported in September that it raised $153 million at a $1 billion valuation in a round led by OpenAI's startup fund, with Founders Fund among the investors. Founders Fund is the venture firm of Peter Thiel, the billionaire co-founder of PayPal and Palantir and the first outside investor in Facebook. Theriva says its stockholders funded about $201.5 million of research from 2010 to 2025; the three programs went for $350,000 up front, of which $140,000 went straight to a royalty holder.

"The AI does not discover the drug. The shareholders paid for it. The AI comes in after the drug is ripped from the people who financed it," Mr. Nejmeh said.

What the filings show

Securities filings show Biossil bought drugs from at least six other drug companies before Theriva. Of those six, two later merged into private companies that went public through them, and one, Applied Therapeutics, was taken private in February 2026 for 8.8 cents a share plus a contingent payment right, with no stockholder vote. Theriva's own filings show its first failed quorum in 20 years of searchable records came on September 21, 2021, the day Mr. Nejmeh attended his first meeting, and that the funds that bought 8.1 million Theriva shares at 54 cents in October 2025 held no common shares at all 17 days later.

"This is Poland on steroids, and the casualties are the dumb money," Mr. Nejmeh said, comparing it to Poland's 1989-91 privatizations, when insiders acquired companies cheaply. Newsweed's earlier story on that pattern is here.

What happens next

Theriva must answer the complaint by December 1. The claims are allegations; no court has ruled on them. None of the firms or people named above, other than Theriva and its four directors, is a party to the case. Mr. Nejmeh's filings are posted at nejmehvstheriva.com.
