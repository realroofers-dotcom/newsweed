Newsweed is going back to paper, the old way and a new way at once. The plan is one printed sheet, in full color, that is two things at once: folded, it’s a newspaper you take home; laid out, it’s the mat on the table while you eat or drink your coffee. Every week it carries one great picture, the kind people fold and keep, and the stories of the town: the game, the dance, the band, the club, the crime, the corruption, the national story everyone is talking about.

The news is behind the QR codes

Each story on the mat is a headline and a line, with a QR code. Scan it and you get a short summary, then Newsweed’s videos on YouTube, the free classifieds and the full story. The sheet stays short; the depth is one scan away.

Printed, and nobody can change it

A story online can be changed overnight without a trace. A printed paper cannot. Once a mat is out, every copy holds the same words from that day, and the record stays.

Made and delivered by local people

Nothing is shipped. Each mat is printed in the city where it is read and delivered by people who live there, as gigs on Gigapoo:

  • Route drivers take the mats to the diners and coffee shops on their route and sell ads to the shops they pass. They keep 10% of every ad, every month it runs. Many will be rideshare and delivery drivers adding a route between trips.
  • Store recruiters sign up the diners and coffee shops that put the mats on their tables.
  • Printers, bundle runners, photographers and local reporters make the rest.

Routes are limited: one driver per territory. When a city’s routes are taken, the rest wait in line.

For shops and local businesses

A shop that puts the mats on its tables gets a month of online advertising in trade. A business that advertises on the mat sits in front of customers for a whole meal, next to its neighbors, and every business on the mat sends customers to the others.
